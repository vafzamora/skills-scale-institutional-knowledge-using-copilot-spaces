# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview

OctoAcme project management processes enable consistent, repeatable delivery across cross-functional teams. The system is grounded in clear principles: customer-first prioritization, iterative delivery of small testable increments, named ownership for every project, and data-informed decision making. Key roles — Project Manager (PM), Product Manager (PdM), Developers, and QA — each have defined responsibilities and communication patterns that keep work transparent and accountable throughout the project lifecycle.

Projects follow a structured lifecycle that begins with Initiation (defining the problem statement, stakeholders, and high-level timeline), moves through Planning (scope, milestones, dependencies, and resource alignment), and then Execution (build, test, review, and iterate). Critical artifacts — including the Project Charter, sprint backlog, acceptance criteria, and risk register — are maintained at every stage to ensure the team has a shared source of truth and a clear definition of done.

Risk management and stakeholder communication are treated as continuous activities, not one-time events. Teams maintain a living risk register, provide weekly status updates, and follow defined escalation paths from the team level up to the project sponsor. Communication templates for status updates and incident response help ensure consistent, timely information sharing with all stakeholder groups.

After each sprint, release, or significant milestone, teams run structured retrospectives to surface what went well and what can be improved. Action items are assigned owners and due dates, tracked in the project backlog, and reviewed at weekly PM syncs. This continuous improvement mindset — measuring impact, celebrating learnings, and making small iterative changes — is what sustains quality and team effectiveness over time.
